# Vet Tech Educational Web App — Content and Safety Policy

**Status:** Draft 1  
**Created:** August 14, 2026

## Purpose and authority

The app supports education and study. It does not replace veterinary authority, supervised clinical instruction, clinic policy, product labeling, or applicable law. Completion of a lesson or quiz is not proof of hands-on competency.

## Risk categories

- **Foundational:** terminology, communication, and documentation concepts.
- **Clinical:** species-aware examination support, husbandry, preventive care, measurements, positioning, and workflows.
- **High-risk:** restraint, venipuncture, injections, vaccines, medication, fluid therapy, anesthesia, radiation safety, emergencies, and invasive procedures.

Risk determines the required review and the prominence of limits and escalation guidance.

## Source standards

Favor, in order appropriate to the subject:

1. Current veterinary guidelines or consensus statements
2. Government and recognized public-health guidance
3. Veterinary schools and teaching hospitals
4. Peer-reviewed literature
5. Current veterinary textbooks or specialist references
6. Product labeling for product-specific facts

Blogs, social posts, forums, and unsourced summaries must not be the sole authority for clinical instruction.

Each lesson records source title, author or organization, date when available, link or citation, access date when relevant, and the claims supported.

## Review requirements

- Foundational content: editorial review and source verification.
- Clinical content: editorial review, source verification, and qualified veterinary-professional review before approval.
- High-risk content: veterinarian or appropriately credentialed veterinary-technician review, relevant subject experience, prerequisites, contraindication categories, stop conditions, supervision requirements, and scheduled re-review.

Unreviewed clinical or high-risk content must not appear as approved guidance.

## Required metadata

- Identifier, title, objectives, audience, and prerequisites
- Species, region limitations, and risk level
- Author and reviewer status
- Status: `draft`, `in_review`, `approved`, `needs_update`, or `retired`
- Last review and next-review target when applicable
- Sources

## Writing rules

Content must:

- Separate client report, direct observation, measurement, and interpretation
- Use precise, neutral language
- Identify species applicability and exceptions
- Explain why a concept matters
- Distinguish recognition from diagnosis
- Direct the learner to stop and seek guidance when appropriate
- Use fictional or properly de-identified examples

Content must not:

- Diagnose a real patient or recommend patient-specific treatment
- Generalize one species' technique to all species
- Encourage work outside authorized scope
- Present dosing without a separately approved safety design
- Hide meaningful disagreement between credible sources
- Equate reading or quiz completion with procedural competency

## Intake and documentation safety

Teach learners to attribute client statements, record units and context, avoid converting theories into diagnoses, ask non-leading questions, escalate concerning findings, and follow clinic record-correction policy.

## Procedural content safety

Before procedural content is published, it must identify:

- Species, patient, and context limits
- Required supervision and competencies
- Equipment and environment considerations
- Reasons not to proceed
- Patient stress and stop criteria
- Human safety considerations
- When the veterinarian or senior technician takes over
- Whether the lesson is conceptual, demonstrative, or for supervised practice

## Vaccines and preventive care

- Identify region and date.
- Distinguish general principles from law, product labeling, and clinic policy.
- Do not imply that all covered species routinely receive vaccines.
- Treat eligibility, product selection, administration, and adverse-event response as veterinarian-directed.

## TPR and reference information

- Explain variation by species, patient, health, stress, environment, and technique.
- Never present one range as a substitute for interpretation.
- Any future reference interval must include species, source, units, context, and review date.
- Unexpected findings should be verified and escalated according to patient condition and clinic policy.

## Privacy

- Do not use identifiable patient, client, employee, or clinic information without explicit authorization and an appropriate legal basis.
- Do not store medical records in the MVP.
- Do not ask learners to paste confidential records into the app.

## Corrections

Questionable content is marked `needs_update` or withdrawn, reviewed against current authoritative sources, reapproved at the required level, and recorded in revision history. Unsafe or obsolete content is retired.

## Educational notice

> This app supports veterinary education. It does not replace supervised training, veterinary direction, clinic protocols, or applicable laws. For patient-specific decisions or concerning findings, stop and consult the attending veterinarian or authorized supervisor.

## Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Objectives match instruction and assessment
- [ ] Species and risk are explicit
- [ ] Claims have recorded sources
- [ ] Client report, observation, measurement, and interpretation remain distinct
- [ ] Escalation and stop guidance is present where necessary
- [ ] No diagnosis or treatment is implied
- [ ] Required professional review is complete
- [ ] Review status and date are accurate
- [ ] Scenarios are fictional or properly de-identified
- [ ] Accessibility and plain-language review are complete
