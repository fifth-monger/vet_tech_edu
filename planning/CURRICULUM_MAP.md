# Vet Tech Educational Web App — Curriculum Map

**Status:** Draft 1  
**Created:** August 14, 2026

## Curriculum model

```text
Learning Track
└── Course
    └── Module
        └── Lesson
            ├── Objectives
            ├── Instruction
            ├── Example or scenario
            ├── Practice
            ├── Knowledge check
            └── Reference card
```

Completion records educational exposure and assessment participation—not hands-on competency.

## Learning levels

1. Recognize a term, observation, or safety concern.
2. Explain the concept and its importance.
3. Apply it in a written or low-risk scenario.
4. Demonstrate the skill under supervision.
5. Maintain professional competency.

The app can assess Levels 1–3 and support, but not independently verify, Levels 4–5.

## Track A: Core foundations

### A1. Medical and anatomical terminology

- Orientation and directional terms
- Regions and major body systems
- Describing location, duration, frequency, distribution, severity, and progression
- Objective description versus interpretation
- Common and potentially ambiguous abbreviations
- Accurate verbal handoff

### A2. Patient intake and examination support

- Presenting complaint and client priorities
- Open-ended questions and focused clarification
- History: onset, progression, appetite, drinking, elimination, activity, behavior, mobility, medications, prior care, diet, environment, exposures, travel, trauma, and toxins
- Client report versus direct observation
- TPR purpose, units, context, reliability, and escalation
- Weight and clinic-approved observations
- Preparing for and supporting the veterinarian's examination

### A3. Medical documentation

- Client-reported information, direct observations, measurements, and veterinary assessment as separate information types
- Specific, concise, neutral language
- Relevant positives and negatives
- Applied intake and verbal-handoff scenarios

### A4. Radiography foundations

- Projection, position, directional terminology, orientation, and markers
- Image-acquisition concepts and patient welfare
- Technical-quality awareness versus veterinary interpretation

### A5. Clinical safety — post-MVP

- Infection prevention and PPE
- Sharps and exposure awareness
- Patient stress and fear signals
- Bites, scratches, zoonotic exposure, and incident escalation
- When not to proceed

## Track B: Avian medicine

Recommended order:

1. Diversity and terminology
2. Anatomy and physiology
3. Husbandry and nutritional history
4. Distant observation and stress recognition
5. Examination support
6. Handling and restraint principles
7. Diagnostics and sample-collection concepts
8. Radiography
9. Preventive medicine
10. Urgent escalation scenarios

## Track C: Reptile medicine

Recommended order:

1. Major groups and terminology
2. Species identification and natural history
3. Temperature, humidity, UVB, enclosure, diet, and supplementation history
4. Distant observation
5. Anatomy and physiology
6. Examination and handling support
7. Diagnostics and sample collection
8. Radiography
9. Preventive medicine
10. Urgent escalation

Reptile content must not generalize one group's requirements to all reptiles.

## Track D: Rabbit medicine

Recommended order:

1. Terminology and anatomy
2. Diet, hay intake, fecal output, housing, and behavior history
3. Distant observation, pain, and stress
4. Examination and handling support
5. Dental and gastrointestinal context
6. Diagnostics and blood-collection concepts
7. Radiography
8. Region-dependent preventive care and vaccination concepts
9. Urgent escalation

## Track E: Rodent medicine

Recommended order:

1. Species distinctions and terminology
2. Anatomy, physiology, and lifespan context
3. Diet, enclosure, bedding, social group, and exposure history
4. Distant observation, respiration, coat, activity, pain, and stress
5. Examination and handling support
6. Diagnostics and sample collection
7. Radiography
8. Preventive medicine
9. Urgent escalation

Rodent lessons must identify the species or applicable group.

## Prerequisites

```text
Medical terminology
├── Patient intake and TPR
├── Documentation
└── Radiography foundations

Intake + documentation
└── Species-specific history and examination support

Terminology + anatomy + clinical safety
├── Handling concepts
├── Sample-collection concepts
└── Radiographic positioning
```

## MVP lesson order

1. Orientation and directional terminology
2. Describing findings objectively
3. Presenting complaint and client communication
4. History collection
5. TPR purpose, documentation, and escalation
6. Subjective versus objective documentation
7. Applied intake and handoff scenario
8. Radiograph vocabulary and role boundaries
9. Optional avian husbandry-history introduction

## Lesson requirements

Every lesson includes:

- Audience, prerequisites, objectives, species, and risk classification
- Short instructional sections and an applied example
- Knowledge check with explanations
- Reference card when appropriate
- Sources, review date, and reviewer status
- Clear escalation language when safety is involved
