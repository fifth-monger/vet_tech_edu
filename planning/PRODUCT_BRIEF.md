# Vet Tech Educational Web App — Product Brief

**Status:** Draft 1  
**Created:** August 14, 2026  
**Owner:** Victoria Garcia

## Product summary

A practical, species-aware educational web app for veterinary technicians and support staff. It will teach foundational clinical terminology, patient intake, TPR, documentation, radiography concepts, and safe clinical workflows, with dedicated tracks for birds, reptiles, rabbits, and rodents.

The app is an educational aid—not a replacement for veterinary direction, supervised clinical training, clinic protocols, or professional judgment.

## Problem

Veterinary technicians must connect a large vocabulary to real clinical situations. Learning material is often scattered across textbooks, workplace instruction, videos, and general references. Exotic companion animals receive less consistent coverage, and advice appropriate for one species can be unsafe for another.

## Intended users

### Primary user

- Victoria, learning and working as a veterinary technician while developing an interest in avian, reptile, rabbit, and rodent medicine

### Future users

- Veterinary technician students
- Newly hired veterinary assistants or technicians
- Working technicians reviewing unfamiliar concepts
- General-practice staff beginning to work with exotic companion animals

The first release is for an individual learner, not clinic-wide training management.

## User needs

The app should help a learner:

- Understand and use medical and anatomical terminology
- Collect a useful client history without diagnosing
- Understand how TPR and initial observations fit into a visit
- Describe observations objectively
- Learn radiograph terminology before advanced positioning or interpretation
- Recognize when to escalate to the veterinarian
- Study within the correct species context
- Practice with explanations rather than memorize answers
- Resume learning and review weak areas
- See sources and content review dates

## Product principles

1. Patient safety before completeness.
2. Species context is required.
3. Teach observation, not unsupported diagnosis.
4. Understanding before memorization.
5. Lessons must work in short study sessions.
6. Clinical sources and review status remain visible.
7. The interface should be accessible, calm, and low-pressure.
8. Prefer portable content and understandable architecture.

## Initial subject areas

### Core foundations

- Medical and anatomical terminology
- Client intake and patient history
- TPR and initial patient observations
- Objective documentation and common abbreviations
- Introductory radiograph terminology

### Specialty tracks

- Avian medicine
- Reptile medicine
- Rabbit medicine
- Rodent medicine

Later specialty lessons may include anatomy, husbandry, handling, examination support, stress and pain indicators, blood-collection concepts, preventive care, radiography, and urgent escalation. They will be introduced according to clinical risk and review readiness.

## Learning loop

1. **Learn:** concise, structured instruction.
2. **Observe:** a realistic example or scenario.
3. **Practice:** label, match, sequence, or apply.
4. **Verify:** a knowledge check with explanations.
5. **Reference:** a concise review card.

## MVP success criteria

A learner can:

- Navigate from the dashboard to a course and lesson
- Complete lessons on terminology, intake, TPR, documentation, and radiograph terminology
- Answer questions and read explanations
- Save and resume progress on the same device
- Review missed concepts
- View sources and review dates
- Use the app comfortably on phone and desktop

## MVP non-goals

- Diagnosing patients or recommending treatment
- Dose calculators or patient-specific decision support
- Replacing hands-on instruction or supervised competency assessment
- Certifying professional competence
- Maintaining medical records
- Accounts, clinic administration, or compliance reporting
- AI-generated clinical guidance presented as authoritative
- A complete procedural or species library

## Open decisions

- Product name and visual identity
- Qualified reviewers for clinical and high-risk content
- When offline installation becomes necessary
- When TypeScript or Tailwind would provide enough value to introduce them
